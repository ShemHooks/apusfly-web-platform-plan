Platform Overview

ApusFly is a drone operating and aerial intelligence platform for coordinating missions, validating operational readiness and compliance, executing and monitoring flights, collecting live mission data, and transforming aerial information into actionable decisions. Service discovery, quotations, and booking are supported workflows—not the primary product thesis. The web application should share a single Next.js codebase with role-specific navigation, dashboards, permissions, and organization-scoped data.

Mission Operations: mission intake, planning, dispatch, live status, telemetry, incident escalation, and after-action review.
Compliance & Readiness: pilot qualifications, aircraft documentation, mission approvals, safety checklists, and evidence trails.
Pilot & Provider Network: affiliations, qualifications, availability, deployment, utilization, and performance.
Aerial Intelligence: authorized live feeds, geospatial outputs, AI-assisted analytics, dashboards, and decision-support reporting.
Enterprise & Government: inspections, monitoring, emergency incident requests, DFR command-center coordination, and accountable reporting.
Commercial Workflows: service discovery, quotations, bookings, billing, and payouts.

Status: proposed functional requirements, not an implementation audit. Telemetry, DFR, live video, AI, and compliance capabilities are planned requirements and depend on integrations, authorizations, and phased delivery.

RBAC and Security Requirements

Platform roles should be assigned through platform-level user-role relationships. Provider roles and organization client roles should be bound to memberships within a specific provider or organization. The same user may hold different roles in different organizations.

Example permission names: platform.users.read, platform.audit.read, platform.missions.dispatch, platform.telemetry.read, platform.finance.refunds.approve, provider.pilots.assign, provider.qualifications.review, provider.drones.update, organization.missions.request, organization.incidents.dispatch, organization.live_feeds.read, organization.after_action_reports.read.

Laravel must enforce every permission and resource ownership rule on the server. Hiding menu items in Next.js is only a user-interface convenience. Sensitive actions require audit trails, and high-risk financial or access changes should use separation of duties. Live telemetry, video feeds, incident records and AI outputs need explicit access policies, retention controls, source attribution, and human review for consequential decisions. Mission approvals must not be treated as proof of regulatory authorization.

The prior RBAC seeders covered 12 roles; the updated 14-role web specification adds Sales / Business Development, Marketing Manager, Auditor, and Technical Support. Align seeders and permission policies before enabling these roles.

Recommended Implementation Roadmap

Phase Roles Primary Goal
1 — Foundation Super Admin, System Admin Authentication, RBAC hardening, users and administration
2 — Providers Owner, Manager, Staff Provider onboarding, pilot network, qualifications, fleet and readiness
3 — Operations Operations Manager, Customer Support Mission intake, dispatch, live monitoring, telemetry, incidents and tickets
4 — Finance Finance / Accounting Bookings, payments, commissions, refunds and payouts
5 — Organizations Enterprise, Government / LGU Mission requests, government/DFR command center, approvals and intelligence reporting
6 — Expansion Sales, Marketing, Auditor, Technical Support Growth, oversight, telemetry/AI analytics, diagnostics and advanced administration

Define all 14 roles early; implement their workflows in phases. Live telemetry and DFR integration depend on aircraft connectivity, secure streaming, regulatory review, and agency operating procedures; AI insights are advisory and require validation. Organization clients will eventually benefit from additional scoped subroles such as Organization Admin, Requester and Approver.
